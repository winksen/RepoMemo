# Plan for the 0.7 line: projects connected to their evidence

Written 2026-11-17 for V0.7.0. Baseline: V0.6.30. Scope is the 0.7.x rows in the [roadmap](../ROADMAP.md#the-07x-line-projects-connected-to-their-evidence) and the [release index](../RELEASES_0.7.md).

## What a project is, and what it is not

A **workspace** stays the security boundary. Everything in this plan lives inside one workspace and is readable by exactly the people who can read that workspace, at the role they already hold. A project never grants access, and nothing here is an access-control substitute: not a project, not a label, not a milestone.

| Concept | Meaning | Lives in | Not to be confused with |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Project** | A named piece of work with an owner, a goal, a lifecycle (`active`, `on_hold`, `done`, `archived`) and an optional target date | A workspace | A workspace, a repository or a tracker project |
| **Brief** | The project's goal text plus the decision memories and evidence it cites, by reference | A project | A copy of the sources: nothing cited is copied |
| **Milestone** | A named, dated group of a project's tasks with a stated completion rule | A project | A task, or a date the tasks follow automatically |
| **Task** | One unit of work with a stable key (`KEY-12`), an assignee, a status, a priority and an optional due date | A workspace, optionally in a project | A checklist step; a tracker issue |
| **Checklist step** | A small step inside one task, with no owner or date of its own | A task | A child task (a child task has its own owner, status and discussion) |
| **Evidence** | A reference to a file, card or memory the task or project relies on | A task or project | The text of that source |
| **Update** | A person's dated statement of project health with links to what changed | A project | A computed score |

Rules that hold for the whole line:

1. **Legacy tasks need no project.** A task created before 0.7, or without a project, keeps working in the workspace task list, in *My work* and in the API.
2. **The same four states** (`open`, `in_progress`, `blocked`, `done`) are the categories every workflow maps to. Existing tasks migrate unchanged.
3. **A reference is checked when it is used.** A link to evidence or a memory is validated against the workspace when saved and re-evaluated against current rights when read; a reader who cannot read the source sees that something is linked, never its text.
4. **Nothing silently reschedules or completes.** Changing a milestone date moves no task date; closing a blocker completes no dependent task; completing a parent hides no child.
5. **A person decides project health.** A project update is written or confirmed by a person; the product may show counts next to it but never sets on-track, at-risk or blocked itself.
6. **Concurrent edits are detected.** Task and project saves carry the version they edited; a stale save is refused with the attempted change returned.

## Excluded on purpose

Gantt charts, critical-path scheduling, resource or capacity accounting, time tracking, budgets, sprints and velocity, cross-workspace portfolios, and custom field types. Rows that look like them (milestones, dependencies, workflows) are deliberately smaller: dates are statements, not schedules. The closing release (V0.7.20) states this again in the guides.

## Coexistence with external trackers

Until 0.8, nothing in a project reads a tracker. From 0.8 a mirrored issue is read-only evidence in a workspace; the remote tracker stays the authority for that issue's state, and a local task that follows it up is a separate record with its own owner. CSV import and export (V0.7.18) is the migration path in and out.

## The sample team journey

A three-person team (Priya owns, Marc and Dana contribute, Lee is a guest reader) moves a service to a new queue:

1. Priya creates the project "Queue migration" with a goal and a target date, and cites the decision memory "Use the managed queue" and the load-test report. (0.7.1, 0.7.2)
2. She plans the milestone "Dual write" with a date and adds tasks `QM-1` to `QM-6`, one of which already existed as a legacy task. (0.7.3, 0.7.7)
3. Marc and Dana discuss `QM-2`, mention each other, and Dana watches `QM-4`. (0.7.4)
4. `QM-5` is blocked by `QM-3`; the list shows why. Closing `QM-3` updates the indication and completes nothing. (0.7.9)
5. Two people edit `QM-2` at once; the second save is refused and its change is kept. (0.7.6)
6. Priya filters by label and milestone, saves the view, moves cards on the board by keyboard and bulk-assigns three tasks with a per-item result. (0.7.10–0.7.14)
7. Overdue work reaches the inbox once; a weekly recurring task creates one occurrence per week. (0.7.15, 0.7.16)
8. Priya publishes "At risk: dual write is two days late" linked to the milestone, the blocker and the load-test report; Lee reads it as a guest and cannot open a source the guest role does not cover. (0.7.17)
9. The team exports a CSV for the tracker, then the whole workspace as an archive, and imports it elsewhere with its schedules paused. (0.7.18, 0.7.19)

Exit check (V0.7.20): the maintainer walks this journey and records each result in the closing record.
